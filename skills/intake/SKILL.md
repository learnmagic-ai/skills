# Intake

Most projects go wrong at the start, not the end. The idea feels clear in your head, but nobody wrote down what "done" means, who it's for, or what's out of bounds. Then halfway through, it drifts.

This skill fixes that with a short, friendly interview. At the end you get a **project brief**: one page that says what you're making, who it's for, what limits you're working within, how you'll know it worked, and what's not included. Anyone (you next week, a friend, or an AI) can read it cold and understand the project.

Start by saying: "Let's pin this project down. I'll ask a few quick questions, one at a time. It takes a few minutes."

---

## Step 1: Work out where the person is starting from

People arrive in one of three ways. Pick the one that fits.

- **A. They've dumped a lot of thoughts** (a long, rambling message, a voice-note-style ramble, a list of fragments). Go to Step 2A.
- **B. They've given one short line, or nothing yet.** Go to Step 2B.
- **C. They've pasted or shared an existing brief** and want it checked. Go to Step 2C.

Also give the project a short name. If one is obvious, use it. If not, ask first: "What's a short name for this project? Something like 'family-recipes' or 'garden-planner' is perfect."

---

## Step 2A: Pull structure out of a brain-dump

Don't run the full interview. Read what they wrote and pull out as much as you can. Then show them what you heard:

```
What I heard
  Goal:         [what you found, or "unclear"]
  Who it's for: [what you found, or "unclear"]
  Limits:       [what you found, or "unclear"]
  Success:      [what you found, or "unclear"]
  In / out:     [what you found, or "unclear"]
```

Then ask only about the parts marked "unclear", one at a time. Never re-ask something they already told you.

---

## Step 2B: The short interview

Ask these one at a time. Skip any they've already answered. Use simple choices where you can, so it's easy to reply.

**1. Goal**
> "In one sentence, what are you trying to make or achieve?"

**2. Who it's for**
> "Who is this for? Pick any:
> a) Just me
> b) My family or friends
> c) My team or workplace
> d) Customers or the public
> e) Someone else. Who?"

Then two quick follow-ups:
> "Who decides when it's done: you, or someone else?"
> "Who's doing the work: you alone, you with AI, or others too?"

**3. Limits**
> "What limits are you working within? Pick any:
> a) A deadline. When?
> b) A budget. Roughly how much?
> c) It has to use a certain tool or app. Which?
> d) It has to work with something you already have. What?
> e) None of these."

**4. Success**
> "How will you know it worked? What will you (or someone else) be able to do or see that you can't today?"

Push gently for something you can actually check. "It's nice" is hard to check. "My sister can find any recipe in under 10 seconds" is easy to check.

**5. In and out**
> "What's definitely NOT part of this, at least for now? For example: a phone version, sharing with others, payments, making it look polished. Or 'nothing ruled out yet'."

Then:
> "And what's definitely IN, that I shouldn't miss?"

---

## Step 2C: Check an existing brief

Read the brief they gave you. Check it has these five parts:

- Goal (one sentence)
- Who it's for (who benefits, who decides, who does the work)
- Limits (time, money, tools)
- Success (things you can check)
- In and out (what's included, what's not)

Show what you found:

```
Brief check
  Goal          - present
  Who it's for  - present
  Limits        - partly (no budget mentioned)
  Success       - present
  In / out      - missing
```

Then ask only about the missing or partial parts, one at a time.

---

## Step 3: Size check

Once you have the five parts, give **one** honest read on how big this is. Don't list options and make them pick. Recommend one:

- **Small:** Something you could finish in one sitting with AI. A single page, a one-off tool, a quick fix. *Next step: just start building, and describe the goal clearly in your first message.*
- **Medium:** A few sessions of work, with several parts that fit together. *Next step: write a simple step-by-step plan first, then build one step at a time.*
- **Large:** Too much to hold in your head at once. Several separate pieces, or weeks of work. *Next step: split it into smaller projects. Pick the smallest piece that's still useful, and run intake again on just that piece.*

Base it on the brief, not on how excited or nervous they sound. If you're torn between two sizes, pick the bigger one and say why. It's safer to over-prepare than to get lost halfway.

Gently flag it if the size and the limits don't match. For example: "This sounds Large, but the deadline is this weekend. Could we shrink the goal to fit?"

Then ask: "Does that feel right to you?" If they disagree, accept their call without arguing and note it in the brief.

---

## Step 4: Final check

Before writing the brief, always ask:

> "Anything I'm missing? Anything you've already decided, or any worry, that should go in the brief?"

Add whatever they say.

If two answers contradict each other ("no deadline" but also "must be done Friday"), point it out and ask which is right. Don't pick one yourself.

---

## Step 5: Write the brief

Write the brief in the chat using this shape:

```
# Project Brief: [name]
Date: [today]

## 1. Goal
[One sentence: what you're making and what it changes.]

## 2. Who it's for
- Who benefits: [...]
- Who decides it's done: [...]
- Who does the work: [...]

## 3. Limits
- Time: [deadline, or "none set"]
- Money: [budget, or "none set"]
- Tools: [required tools or apps, or "none"]
- Depends on: [people or things this needs, or "none"]

## 4. Success
- [ ] [Something you can check]
- [ ] [Something you can check]
- [ ] [Something you can check]

## 5. In and out
In:
- [...]
Out (for now):
- [...]

## 6. Size
[Small / Medium / Large]: [one line on why]
Next step: [the matching next step from Step 3]
```

Rules for writing it:
- **Never invent answers.** If they didn't say, write "Not decided yet". Don't fill gaps with guesses.
- If they said "skip this" or "doesn't matter", write "Deliberately left open". Don't silently drop it.
- The brief must make sense on its own, to someone who never saw this chat. No "as you mentioned above."

**Saving it (optional):** If you are in a tool that can save files, offer to save it as `brief.md` in their project folder. Say where it will go and wait for a yes. In a normal chat, the brief in the conversation is the finished product. Suggest they copy it somewhere safe, since they'll want it when they start planning or building.

---

## Things to keep in mind

- **One question at a time.** Never fire off the whole list like a form.
- **Keep it light.** This should feel like a friend helping you think, not paperwork.
- **Don't re-ask** what they've already told you.
- **Always do the "anything I'm missing?" check** before writing.
- **Stop at the brief.** Intake ends when the brief is written.

---

## What this skill does not do

- It does not plan the project step by step. It captures *what* and *why*, not *how*.
- It does not build anything.
- It does not make decisions for the person. Where they haven't decided, the brief says so.
- It does not look up anything or read files the person didn't share.
- It does not judge whether the idea is good. For that, run a premortem on the plan once you have one.
